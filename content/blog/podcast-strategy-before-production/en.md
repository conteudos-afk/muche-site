The most common reason brand podcasts fail is not poor production or bad content. It is the absence of strategy. They are launched without a clear answer to who they are for, what they are trying to achieve, and how they will sustain themselves beyond the first three episodes. These are questions that must be answered before a microphone is ever switched on.

## Audience before topic

Most podcasts are defined by their topic: a show about marketing, a show about entrepreneurship, a show about wellness. The better approach is to define the audience first and then find the angle on the topic that is most useful and distinctive to that specific group of people. The more precisely you can describe your listener, the more precisely you can serve them.

## Format is a commitment

Interview shows, solo narrative, roundtable, documentary — each format has different production demands, different audience expectations, and different creative possibilities. The format you choose is a commitment you will live with for as long as the show runs. Choose it based on what you can sustain and what best serves the content, not based on what seems most impressive.

## Cadence sets expectations

Weekly, fortnightly, monthly — whatever cadence you commit to, your audience will internalise it. Breaking that cadence without warning erodes trust. Before launching, be honest about your capacity to produce episodes consistently over time. A fortnightly show maintained for two years is worth more than a weekly show that goes dark after six months.

## The first ten episodes are the hardest

Podcast growth is slow and non-linear. Most shows that become successful did not feel successful in the first three months. The brands that succeed with podcasting are the ones that commit to a long enough window to find their voice, build their audience, and refine their format. Twelve months is a minimum. Two years is where the real returns begin to compound.

A podcast is a significant investment of time and creative energy. Approached with strategy, it is one of the most powerful brand-building tools available. Approached without it, it is an expensive experiment that produces three episodes and a lesson learned the hard way.
